# Da Mario — the story of the Funghi

## Where it comes from

The Funghi is from the chestnut woods above Cuneo in Piedmont, where Mario's wife Elena grew up and where her family has picked mushrooms on the same slope since before anyone wrote it down.

Elena's family picks on the same slope above Chiusa Pesio every autumn, and the rule there is older than the family: you take what is in front of you and you leave the mycelium alone. In October and November we write the name of the wood on the day's board, and in the other ten months the mushrooms are cultivated on the same soil by a cooperative two valleys away.

## What the creator had in mind

Elena disliked the mushroom pizzas she was served in cities: pale, wet, sliced from a tin. Her idea was to treat the mushroom like meat — browned separately, salted early, given its own pan — and to put it on the dough already cooked.

The second idea is quieter and it is about water. A raw mushroom is about 90 % water, and all of it leaves in the oven. Put raw mushrooms on a pizza and you are not baking a pizza, you are steaming one from above.

## Why these ingredients

In autumn the mushrooms come from the woods themselves; the rest of the year they are cultivated on the same soil. The pizza is deliberately plain otherwise, because a good mushroom needs no argument.

## How we prepare it

The mushrooms are sauteed in olive oil with a little thyme until they have given up their water and taken colour. They are cooled before they touch the dough, which stops the base from steaming.

They are sliced at four millimetres, cooked in a wide pan in two batches, and salted only once they have taken colour — salt early and they poach in their own liquid instead of browning. A sprig of thyme goes into the pan and comes out again before the mushrooms touch the dough.

## How we bake it

430 degrees, 80 seconds. Mushrooms added raw would release water into the cheese; this is why we cook them first and why our Funghi is drier than most.

The finished pizza is drier than most, which is deliberate, and it wants a thread of olive oil on the way out to bring it back.

## In numbers

- dough ball: 250 g
- mushrooms: 110 g raw, about 70 g once cooked
- sliced at: 4 mm, sauteed in two batches, salted after colouring
- tomato: 80 g · mozzarella: 90 g
- oven floor: 430 °C, bake 80 seconds
- autumn: forest mushrooms; the rest of the year: cultivated, same recipe

## What guests ask

**“Are the mushrooms fresh or from a tin?”**

Fresh, always, and cooked here in a pan before they go on the dough. We have never used tinned mushrooms and the recipe would not survive them.

**“Is the Funghi vegetarian?”**

Yes — tomato, mozzarella, mushrooms. It is not vegan, because of the mozzarella.

**“Which mushrooms are they?”**

In October and November, whatever the wood above Chiusa Pesio gave that week; it is written on the board. Otherwise cultivated mushrooms from a cooperative in the same valley.

## How it goes wrong

- raw mushrooms on the dough: the pizza steams and the base never crisps
- salted at the start of the pan: they poach grey instead of browning
- cooked mushrooms put on hot: they carry their own heat into the cheese and it splits
- one crowded pan instead of two: the same failure as salting early

## House note

In October we write the name of the wood on the day's board. It is the only pizza whose story changes with the season.

Elena still comes into the kitchen in October to look at the first pan of the season. She has never once said it was right the first time.
