# Da Mario — the story of the Verdure

## Where it comes from

The Verdure is the newest recipe on our menu and the only one invented in Saint-Etienne. It was written in 2019 for a regular guest who had stopped eating dairy and stopped coming.

It is the newest recipe in the book and the only one invented in Saint-Étienne. A regular guest stopped eating dairy in 2019 and quietly stopped coming; Elena noticed after about six weeks, called her, and spent a fortnight building a pizza rather than an apology.

## What the creator had in mind

The idea was not to remove the cheese from an existing pizza but to build a pizza that never wanted cheese in the first place. What replaces it is oil, oregano and the sweetness of vegetables roasted until their edges darken.

The decision that made it work was refusing to start from the Margherita. A pizza with the cheese removed tastes of what is missing; a pizza that never wanted cheese does not. What replaces it is oil, oregano and vegetables roasted until their edges darken.

## Why these ingredients

Aubergine, courgette, pepper and red onion all caramelise, and caramelised vegetables give the sweetness and depth that mozzarella otherwise provides. The oregano goes in at the end, where basil would be on a Margherita.

## How we prepare it

The vegetables are roasted in the pizza oven itself, in a steel tray at the mouth, while service runs. They are salted after roasting, never before, so that they colour instead of steaming.

The vegetables are roasted in the pizza oven itself, in a steel tray at the mouth, while service runs — aubergine and courgette in one tray, pepper and red onion in another, because they do not finish at the same time. They are salted after roasting, never before, so they colour instead of steaming.

## How we bake it

430 degrees, 80 seconds. Without cheese the base browns faster, as on the Marinara, so it comes out early.

Without cheese the base browns faster, as on the Marinara, so the pizza comes out early and the cook has to remember that it is not a Vegetariana.

## In numbers

- dough ball: 250 g, no cheese
- aubergine: 40 g · courgette: 40 g · pepper: 40 g · red onion: 30 g
- roasted: in the pizza oven mouth, salted afterwards
- tomato: 80 g · olive oil: 10 g before, 6 g after · oregano: at the end
- oven floor: 430 °C, bake 80 seconds
- vegan — and not advertised in large letters, at the guest's request

## What guests ask

**“Is it vegan?”**

Yes. No dairy, no egg, nothing from an animal. The Marinara is the other vegan pizza on the menu.

**“What is the difference to the Ortolana?”**

One thing: the mozzarella. The Ortolana has it, this one never did. It is the example the kitchen uses when two pizzas look the same and are not.

**“Why is it not marked as vegan on the menu?”**

Because the guest it was invented for asked us not to make a banner of it. She wanted a pizza, not a category.

## How it goes wrong

- vegetables salted before roasting: they steam and stay pale
- aubergine and pepper in the same tray: one is done while the other is raw
- the same bake time as a Vegetariana: without cheese this base browns faster
- oregano added before the bake: it burns, and there is no cheese to shelter it

## House note

The Verdure is vegan and we do not write that word on the menu in large letters, because the guest it was invented for asked us not to.

She came back. She orders it about twice a month and has never once mentioned that it was made for her.
