# Da Mario — the story of the Diavola

## Where it comes from

The Diavola is Calabrian in spirit and comes to us from Spilinga, a village on the Monte Poro plateau known for a spreadable chilli sausage that is either the best thing in the south or an assault, depending on who is asked.

Spilinga sits above the sea on the Monte Poro plateau and holds a festival for its chilli sausage every August. The recipe here is not the village's — theirs is spread on bread, not baked — but the balance of heat to fat is copied from it directly, and Mario has never claimed the idea was his.

## What the creator had in mind

Mario's idea was heat that arrives late. The chilli is not meant to hit the first bite — it should build over the slice, so the pizza is still edible at the end. That is why the chilli is infused into oil instead of scattered raw.

Late heat has a practical reason as well as a pleasant one: a pizza that is aggressive in the first bite is a pizza that gets left, and half a pizza left on the plate is a guest who does not come back.

## Why these ingredients

Calabria is hot and dry and grows chillies that dry well; the region built a cuisine around preserving meat with them. The sausage and the chilli on this pizza come from the same tradition and were never separate ideas.

## How we prepare it

Chilli flakes are steeped in warm olive oil for two hours and the oil is used, not the flakes. The sausage goes on before the bake; a second thread of chilli oil after it.

The chilli oil is made once a week and kept in a dark bottle: a hot infusion with the flakes strained out after two hours. Oil left standing on the flakes turns bitter within a day, and the difference is obvious to anyone who has tasted both.

## How we bake it

430 degrees, 85 seconds. Chilli scorches quickly, and burnt chilli is bitter rather than hot, which is the failure mode to watch for.

A thread of chilli oil goes on after the bake, and the cook may add a second. Never a third — at that point the pizza is an endurance test and the kitchen is not in that business.

## In numbers

- dough ball: 250 g
- sausage: 55 g, sliced at 2 mm
- chilli oil: 6 g before the bake, 6 g after
- tomato: 80 g · mozzarella: 90 g
- infusion: chilli flakes in warm olive oil for 2 hours, then strained
- oven floor: 430 °C, bake 85 seconds

## What guests ask

**“How hot is it, really?”**

Warm rather than fierce, and the heat arrives towards the end of the slice. That is the recipe. If you want more, say so before it goes into the oven.

**“Can you make it hotter?”**

Yes, one further thread of chilli oil. We stop there, on purpose.

**“Is the sausage the same as on the Pepperoni?”**

No. This one is Calabrian, cured with chilli; the Pepperoni's is an American sausage cured with paprika and no heat at all.

## How it goes wrong

- chilli flakes scattered raw: they scorch in the oven and turn bitter
- oil left standing on the flakes for days: bitter before it is ever used
- three threads of oil because a guest asked: nobody finishes it
- the same bake time as a Margherita: this pizza carries more fat and needs the extra ten seconds

## House note

We ask guests who want it hotter to say so before the bake, not after. The oil goes on twice, never three times.

The kitchen keeps a small jug of the plain oil beside the chilli one, because a cook reaching for the wrong bottle at speed is the one mistake on this page that nobody notices until the guest does.
