# Da Mario — the story of the Ortolana

## Where it comes from

The Ortolana takes its name from the word for a market gardener and comes from the Piana del Sele in Campania, the same plain as the buffalo, where vegetables are grown between the dairies.

The Piana del Sele grows vegetables between the buffalo dairies, on land that was swamp until the 1930s. Aubergine and courgette are picked small there, and we buy the smallest we can get from a wholesaler who thinks we are difficult about it.

## What the creator had in mind

The idea is a middle way between the Vegetariana and the Verdure: the cheese stays, but the vegetables are Mediterranean rather than northern — aubergine and pepper rather than mushroom and onion — and olive oil is used as a topping, not only as a dressing.

It sits deliberately between the Vegetariana and the Verdure: the cheese stays, but the vegetables are Mediterranean rather than northern, and olive oil is treated as a topping rather than a dressing. Three vegetable pizzas on one menu is not indecision — it is three different answers to what a guest means by *something with vegetables*.

## Why these ingredients

Aubergine and courgette need heat and dry soil; both grow within a few kilometres of each other on that plain, and both are picked small there, which is why we buy the smallest we can get.

## How we prepare it

Aubergine slices are salted for thirty minutes and pressed before grilling — the step that keeps the pizza from turning bitter. The olive oil goes on in a spiral after the bake.

Aubergine slices are salted for thirty minutes and pressed before grilling. That step is what keeps the pizza from turning bitter, and it is the one a hurried kitchen skips first. The olive oil goes on in a spiral after the bake, with a coarse pepper mill.

## How we bake it

430 degrees, 85 seconds.

The vegetables arrive already grilled, so the bake is a Margherita's with a little more time for the oil to settle.

## In numbers

- dough ball: 250 g
- aubergine: 45 g, salted 30 minutes, pressed, grilled
- courgette: 40 g, grilled dry · pepper: 40 g, roasted and peeled
- tomato: 80 g · mozzarella: 90 g · olive oil: 8 g, in a spiral after the bake
- oven floor: 430 °C, bake 85 seconds
- one ingredient separates it from the Verdure: the mozzarella

## What guests ask

**“What is the difference to the Verdure?”**

The mozzarella, and nothing else of consequence. The Ortolana has it, the Verdure never did — which is why one is vegetarian and the other is vegan.

**“Why is the aubergine salted first?”**

To draw out the liquid that makes it bitter and soggy. Thirty minutes and a weight, and it is a different vegetable afterwards.

**“Is it vegetarian?”**

Yes. Vegetarian, not vegan; the mozzarella is what makes that distinction.

## How it goes wrong

- aubergine grilled unsalted: bitter, and it soaks up oil like a sponge
- vegetables cut thick to save time: they never cook through on a 85-second bake
- the oil spiral before the bake: it fries the toppings instead of dressing them
- confusing it with the Verdure on the pass: one guest gets cheese they did not want
