# Da Mario — the story of the Siciliana

## Where it comes from

The Siciliana comes from Trapani on the west coast of Sicily, where the salt pans and the anchovy boats have shared the same harbour for centuries.

Trapani's salt pans and its anchovy boats have shared a harbour for centuries, and the town preserves everything it catches or grows, because there was salt and sun and no ice. Mario buys the anchovies in salt, not oil, and rinses them here.

## What the creator had in mind

The idea is a pizza that tastes of the sea without any fish on it that looks like fish. The anchovy dissolves in the heat and leaves salt and depth behind, which is exactly what Mario wanted from a pizza served at the end of a long shift.

A pizza that tastes of the sea without anything on it that looks like fish. In the oven the anchovy dissolves into the cheese and leaves salt and depth behind — which is why guests who say they dislike anchovies often finish this one without noticing what they have eaten.

## Why these ingredients

Anchovies, capers and olives are the three things Trapani preserves best, and all three are preserved in salt for the same reason: there was salt, and there was sun, and there was no ice.

## How we prepare it

Capers are rinsed twice and dried; unrinsed capers make the pizza inedible and it is the mistake every new cook makes once. The anchovy fillets are laid last and never chopped.

Capers are rinsed twice in cold water and dried on a cloth; unrinsed capers make the pizza inedible and it is the mistake every new cook makes exactly once. The anchovy fillets are laid whole and last, never chopped, so the cook can see how many went on.

## How we bake it

430 degrees, 80 seconds. The anchovies melt into the cheese during the bake, so the finished pizza shows olives and capers but almost no fish.

Everything on this pizza is already salty, so the dough is the only thing in the bake that can be judged by taste. We salt nothing at the pass.

## In numbers

- dough ball: 250 g
- anchovy: 6 fillets, salt-packed, rinsed here, laid whole
- capers: 12 g, rinsed twice, dried
- olives: 25 g, stoned by hand
- tomato: 80 g · mozzarella: 90 g
- oven floor: 430 °C, bake 80 seconds
- added salt: none, anywhere

## What guests ask

**“I do not like anchovies — is it very fishy?”**

Less than you expect. They dissolve in the bake and what is left is salt and depth. Guests who say this often finish the pizza; ask for one slice before you decide.

**“Is it vegetarian?”**

No. It has anchovies, which is also why the knowledge base does not list it as vegetarian even though it has no meat on it — *free of meat* and *vegetarian* were never the same question.

**“Can I have it without capers?”**

Yes. Then it is saltier rather than milder, because the capers are the sour thing that balances it.

## How it goes wrong

- capers straight from the jar: the pizza is inedible and the brine kills everything else
- chopped anchovies: nobody can tell how many went on, including the cook
- salting the tomato as usual: three of the five toppings are already salt
- oil-packed anchovies used straight: they bring their own oil and the base floods

## House note

We never add salt to this pizza. Three of its five toppings are already salt.

A new cook makes the caper mistake once, tastes it, and never makes it again. We have stopped warning people in advance, because being told is not the same as tasting it.
