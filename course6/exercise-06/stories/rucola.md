# Da Mario — the story of the Rucola

## Where it comes from

The Rucola is from Bari in Apulia, where rocket grows wild by the roadside and where a cook told Mario that anything put into an oven that did not need to go there is a waste of an oven.

Rocket grows wild by the roadside around Bari, and the cook who taught Mario this pizza was contemptuous of anything put into an oven that did not need to go there. He served it on a wooden board with a lemon half, which we do not, because the lemon fights the parmesan.

## What the creator had in mind

A pizza in two temperatures. The base is hot, the topping is cold and raw, and the contrast is the dish. The bitterness of the rocket is not a problem to be cooked away; it is the reason the pizza exists.

A pizza in two temperatures. The base is hot, the topping is cold and raw, and the contrast is the dish. The bitterness of the rocket is not a fault to be cooked away — it is the reason the pizza exists, and it is why the parmesan has to be salty enough to answer it.

## Why these ingredients

Rocket is peppery and bitter, parmesan is salty and sweet, and Apulian olive oil is grassy enough to tie them together. All three are raw on the finished pizza.

## How we prepare it

The rocket is washed, dried completely and kept cold until the pizza is out of the oven. Wet rocket wilts on contact and the pizza looks defeated before it reaches the table.

The rocket is washed, spun and dried completely, then kept cold until the pizza is out of the oven. Wet rocket wilts on contact and the pizza looks defeated before it reaches the table. The parmesan is shaved with a peeler, never grated: grated parmesan melts into the heat and disappears.

## How we bake it

430 degrees, 75 seconds as a plain Margherita without basil. Rocket and parmesan are added after the oven, always, and shaved parmesan means shaved — not grated.

The pizza baked in the oven is a Margherita without basil. Everything that makes it a Rucola happens in the fifteen seconds afterwards.

## In numbers

- dough ball: 250 g
- tomato: 80 g · mozzarella: 90 g
- rocket: 30 g, washed, spun, dried, kept cold
- parmesan: 20 g, shaved with a peeler, 30 months aged
- olive oil: 6 g, grassy, after the bake
- oven floor: 430 °C, bake 75 seconds — rocket and parmesan go on afterwards

## What guests ask

**“Is the rocket cooked?”**

No, and it must not be. It goes on after the oven, cold, and the heat of the pizza is all it gets.

**“Why shaved parmesan and not grated?”**

Grated parmesan melts into the heat and vanishes. Shavings stay themselves, which is the contrast the pizza is built on.

**“Is it vegetarian?”**

Yes. Tomato, mozzarella, rocket, parmesan, oil.

## How it goes wrong

- rocket added before the bake: it turns black and the pizza is finished
- wet rocket: it wilts on contact and slides off the slice
- grated parmesan: it disappears and the pizza tastes of nothing but bitter leaves
- rocket that has been sitting at room temperature: limp before it ever reaches the pizza
