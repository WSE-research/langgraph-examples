# Da Mario — the story of the Capricciosa

## Where it comes from

The Capricciosa is from Rome, from the years when a pizzeria wrote the day's leftovers on a board and called the result a whim. Mario's version comes from a trattoria near Campo de' Fiori that has since become a phone shop.

The trattoria near Campo de' Fiori wrote the day's leftovers on a slate and called the result *capricciosa*, the whimsical one. It closed in 2011. Mario ate there four times and asked for the recipe twice; the second time he was told there was no recipe, which he now considers the recipe.

## What the creator had in mind

The idea is a pizza with four corners of flavour that never mix into a mush: salty ham, sour artichoke, earthy mushroom, rich egg. Each quarter is dressed separately so the guest can choose what a bite contains.

The quarters are the whole design. Scatter the same six toppings evenly and every bite is a compromise; keep them apart and the guest decides what a bite contains. It is the only pizza in the house that is served with a serving suggestion.

## Why these ingredients

Artichokes come from the coast north of Rome, where they are grown in sandy soil and preserved in oil; olives and egg were what a Roman kitchen always had. The combination is an inventory, not a design — and it works anyway.

## How we prepare it

Toppings are placed in quarters, not scattered. The egg is cracked into the centre and only the yolk is protected with a spoon of tomato around it.

Artichokes are drained and pressed lightly, mushrooms arrive cooked from the same pan as the Funghi, olives are stoned by hand in the morning, and the ham is folded rather than laid flat. The egg is cracked into the centre with a spoon of tomato banked around the yolk to protect it.

## How we bake it

420 degrees, 95 seconds. The egg white must set while the yolk stays soft, which is the whole difficulty of this pizza and the reason it is never rushed.

The white must set while the yolk stays soft, and the difference between the two states is about fifteen seconds. Cooks judge it by the white's edge, not by the clock.

## In numbers

- dough ball: 250 g
- four quarters: ham, artichoke, mushroom, olive
- egg: 1, cracked into the centre, yolk banked with tomato
- tomato: 80 g · mozzarella: 90 g
- oven floor: 420 °C, bake 95 seconds
- olives: stoned by hand in the morning, never bought stoned

## What guests ask

**“Can I have it without the egg?”**

Yes, and the cook will ask once whether you are sure. Without the egg it is close to a Quattro Stagioni.

**“What is the difference to the Quattro Stagioni?”**

The egg, and the arrangement. The Stagioni's quarters are a calendar laid in the order of the year; this one's are simply kept apart.

**“Are the artichokes fresh?”**

Preserved in oil, from the coast north of Rome. Fresh artichokes on a pizza go grey and woody in the heat.

## How it goes wrong

- toppings scattered instead of quartered: the design is gone and so is the point
- the yolk left unprotected: it sets hard and the pizza loses its centre
- artichokes straight from the jar: the oil pools and the base softens
- olives bought ready-stoned: they are soft, salty and taste of brine

## House note

Order it without the egg and the kitchen will make it, but the cook will ask you once whether you are sure.

Guests who eat it clockwise starting at the ham are doing it the way the Roman cook showed Mario. Nobody will correct you if you do not.
